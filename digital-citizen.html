1 What is a digital citizen?

A digital citizen is someone who uses the internet and digital technology in a responsible, respectful, and safe way. Just like being a good citizen in your community means following rules and treating others well, being a good digital citizen means doing the same thing online.

Every time you go online — whether you're playing a game, watching videos, chatting with friends, or doing homework — you are participating in a digital community. And like any community, it works best when everyone does their part.

💡 Key idea: Being a digital citizen is about more than just using technology. It's about using it wisely, kindly, and safely. You are responsible for your actions online, just like you are offline.

2 The 9 elements of digital citizenship

Experts have identified nine key areas that make up digital citizenship. Understanding each one helps you become a better, safer, and more responsible digital citizen.

Element What it means
1. Digital Access Everyone should have fair access to technology and the internet.
2. Digital Commerce Buying and selling online safely and legally.
3. Digital Communication Communicating respectfully and clearly through digital tools.
4. Digital Literacy Learning how to find, evaluate, and use information online.
5. Digital Etiquette Following rules of good behavior and manners online.
6. Digital Law Understanding the legal rules of using technology.
7. Digital Rights & Responsibilities You have rights online — and you also have responsibilities.
8. Digital Health & Wellness Balancing screen time with physical and mental health.
9. Digital Security Protecting your devices, accounts, and personal information.

3 Digital etiquette (netiquette)

Digital etiquette — often called netiquette — is about treating others with respect online. Just because you're behind a screen doesn't mean your words and actions don't affect real people.

Good netiquette vs. bad netiquette

✅ Good netiquette

• Think before you post

• Use kind and respectful language

• Respect others' opinions

• Give credit when you use others' work

• Report bullying instead of joining in

• Ask permission before posting photos of others

❌ Bad netiquette

• Posting mean comments

• Using ALL CAPS to shout

• Sharing someone's private info

• Spreading rumors or fake news

• Cyberbullying

• Posting embarrassing photos without consent

💡 The Golden Rule online: Treat others online the way you want to be treated. If you wouldn't say it to someone's face, don't type it online.

4 Cyberbullying — what it is and how to stop it

Cyberbullying is when someone uses digital technology to hurt, embarrass, threaten, or harass another person. It can happen through text messages, social media, online games, or any digital platform.

Examples of cyberbullying

  • Sending mean or threatening messages
  • Posting embarrassing photos or videos of someone
  • Spreading rumors online
  • Excluding someone from an online group on purpose
  • Creating fake accounts to impersonate someone
  • Leaving hurtful comments on someone's posts

What to do if you see cyberbullying

  • Don't respond — reacting often makes it worse.
  • Save the evidence — take screenshots as proof.
  • Report it — use the platform's report button.
  • Block the person — stop them from contacting you.
  • Tell a trusted adult — a parent, teacher, or school counselor.
  • Support the victim — let them know they're not alone.

⚠️ Remember: Cyberbullying is never the victim's fault. If you are being bullied online, it is okay to ask for help. You deserve to feel safe.

5 Your digital footprint

Your digital footprint is the trail of data you leave behind every time you go online. It includes everything you post, comment, like, search, and share. This footprint can last a very long time — sometimes forever.

Two types of digital footprints

Type Description Examples
Active footprint Data you intentionally share Posts, comments, photos, likes, shares
Passive footprint Data collected without you actively sharing Websites visited, location data, search history

Why your digital footprint matters

  • Colleges and employers may look at your online presence.
  • Scholarships can be affected by what you post.
  • Your reputation — online actions can shape how others see you.
  • Permanence — even deleted posts can be saved by others.
💡 Think before you post: Ask yourself these questions:
• Would I be okay if my parents or teachers saw this?
• Would I be okay if this was shown on the news?
• Will this still be okay in 5 years?
• Am I being kind and respectful?

6 Your digital rights and responsibilities

As a digital citizen, you have rights — things you are entitled to — and responsibilities — things you are expected to do.

Your digital rights

  • The right to privacy and to control your personal information.
  • The right to express yourself freely (within legal limits).
  • The right to be safe from harassment and bullying.
  • The right to access information and technology.
  • The right to have your creative work protected.

Your digital responsibilities

  • Treat others online with respect and kindness.
  • Protect your own personal information and accounts.
  • Respect others' privacy — don't share their info without permission.
  • Follow the law and the rules of the platforms you use.
  • Give credit to others' work and avoid plagiarism.
  • Report harmful content and cyberbullying.

7 Digital health and wellness

Being a good digital citizen also means taking care of your own health and well-being. Too much screen time can affect your sleep, your mood, your grades, and your physical health.

Tips for healthy technology use

  • Set limits on screen time — and stick to them.
  • Take regular breaks from screens (try the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds).
  • Don't use screens for at least 1 hour before bedtime.
  • Balance online activities with offline hobbies, sports, and time with friends.
  • Be mindful of how social media makes you feel — if it makes you anxious or sad, step away.
  • Remember that what you see online is often a highlight reel, not reality.

💡 Remember: Technology is a tool. You control it — it should not control you. Healthy habits online lead to a healthier, happier life overall.

8 Key facts to remember

• A digital citizen uses technology responsibly, respectfully, and safely.

• There are 9 elements of digital citizenship, including access, etiquette, and security.

• Netiquette means being polite and respectful online.

• Cyberbullying is using technology to hurt others — report it and tell a trusted adult.

• Your digital footprint is the trail of data you leave online.

• You have rights online (privacy, safety) and responsibilities (respect, kindness).

• Take care of your digital health — balance screen time with offline activities.

• The Golden Rule applies online: treat others the way you want to be treated.
